## English check

1. 1,70,000 Indians, trapped in the middle of a war. The one who got them out wasn't a film hero... it was a man from Kumbanad called "Toyota Sunny".
2. You see: August 2nd. Iraq's tanks rolled into Kuwait. A country vanished overnight. No bank. No salary. No flights.
3. Mathunny Mathews. Landed by ship in '56, became manager at the Toyota company, retired a year earlier.
4. People asked: "A retired car manager, taking on Saddam?"
5. But Sunny, Harbhajan Singh Vedi and friends met the Indian ambassador in Baghdad and sealed a deal with Iraqi bus operators.
6. The buses set off. Basra. Baghdad. Then across the desert to Amman in Jordan. More than a thousand kilometres!
7. Air India was waiting there. Two months. 488 flights. A record that went into the Guinness Book.
8. Like putting a whole town on buses, driving it across a desert and flying it home.
9. Wait... heard this story somewhere? "Airlift". Akshay Kumar's Ranjit Katyal. But that hero is fictional, a character built from Sunny and Vedi.
10. What if those buses had never left? Lakhs of people in a war zone with no money or food. In January, the bombs started falling there.
11. In the film there was one hero... in real life, it was a Toyota Sunny and a lot of ordinary people.

## Fact check

- **2 August 1990 invasion.** "The invasion of Kuwait started on August 2, 1990." (Wikipedia, airlift article.)
- **About 1,70,000 evacuated, 488 flights, Air India with Indian Airlines; Guinness record** for most people evacuated by a civil airliner. (Wikipedia, airlift article.)
- **"Two months".** Sources disagree on the exact window: the airlift article gives 13 Aug–20 Oct in the infobox and "18 August to 20 October… 63 days" in the body; the Mathunny Mathews article says 13 Aug–11 Oct. So the script says "two months", not a day count.
- **Mathunny Mathews ("Toyota Sunny").** Born c. 1936 in Eraviperoor, Kumbanad (Pathanamthitta); reached Kuwait by ship in 1956; nickname from the Toyota agency (Al-Sayer Group), where he retired as Managing Director in 1989 (so "a year earlier" fits). Died 20 May 2017 in Kuwait. (Wikipedia.) The script says "manager", a simplification of Managing Director.
- **What he did.** He and Harbhajan Singh Vedi "played a stellar role in co-ordinating the evacuation operation by meeting the Indian ambassador in Baghdad and overseeing the transport… to Amman via Baghdad by bus after striking a deal with Iraqi transporters, Indian authorities and the UN." Other organisers named: Abey Varicad, N.V.K. Warrier, Ali Hussain, Narinder Singh Sethi. (Wikipedia.) The line keeps "and friends" so it doesn't overclaim a one-man rescue.
- **Route.** Buses ran "through Basra and Baghdad to Amman"; flights went Amman to Bombay (4,117 km). Kuwait → Baghdad → Amman by road is well over 1,000 km (my estimate from the map; no source gives the road distance), so the line says "more than a thousand kilometres". **Check:** if you want a firm number, I'll look for a sourced one.
- **The film.** Airlift (2016, Raja Krishna Menon, Akshay Kumar). Ranjit Katyal is "a fictional character based on both Mathunny Mathews and Harbajan Singh Vedi." Ex-diplomats (K.P. Fabian, Nirupama Rao) criticised it as short on facts. (Wikipedia.)
- **January bombing.** The coalition air campaign over Iraq and occupied Kuwait began on 17 January 1991 (well-known; I haven't re-checked a source in this pass).
- **"Lakhs of people" in the what-if** is the what-if, not a claim. Exact Malayali share of evacuees: no source gives one, so the script doesn't claim it.
- **Left out on purpose:** the famous I.K. Gujral–Saddam hug (not in the sources I read this pass), and "Sunny saved everyone alone".
