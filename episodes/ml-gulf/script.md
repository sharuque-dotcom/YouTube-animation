## English check

1. The 1960s. No passports. A bunch of young Malayali men on a smuggler's launch. Before reaching land, they're dropped into the sea: "Swim the rest!" "Gafoor ka dost" wasn't just a movie joke. This really happened. That swim built today's Kerala.
2. You see, under the sand across the sea was oil. In 1973 the oil price quadrupled. The Gulf got rich overnight. It needed one thing: workers.
3. People back home laughed: "What work in a desert? Herding camels?"
4. But first came a letter. Then a bank draft. Then a TV... and a concrete mansion where the tiled house stood!
5. Today twenty-two lakh Malayalis live abroad, four in five of them in the Gulf. In 2023 alone, ₹2,16,000 crore came home.
6. Of every four rupees Kerala earns, about one comes from across the sea. Like a house running on the neighbour's electricity meter. The lights stay on... until the fuse blows.
7. Wait... has that fuse ever blown? Yes. In 1990 Iraq seized Kuwait. Air India flew 1,70,000 Indians home, many of them Malayalis with nothing in their hands.
8. So what if the Gulf had never happened? No Gulf houses. No Gulf gold. No mothers waiting at the airport.
9. Instead: a land of educated people with no jobs. Queues at the employment exchange stretching for kilometres.
10. If those boys hadn't jumped into the sea that night... Kerala would still be standing on the shore, waiting.

## The Gafoor nod (v2)

Nadodikkattu (1987): Gafoor puts Dasan and Vijayan on a launch to "Dubai", has them wade ashore, and they find out they're in Madras. "Gafoor ka dost" is the line every Malayali knows. The script uses it as the snapback: the joke everyone laughs at was a real journey for real boys.

## Fact check

- **The swim (line 1).** Documented in oral histories and journalism, not a single named voyage: 1960s "pioneers" left on a *kalla kappal* (country boat) without documents; the dhow "would drop them off far away from the shore and they would have to swim across the sea for the last bit." (The News Minute, Gita Aravamudan, 2020.) Also the subject of the film Pathemari. I kept it unnamed and plural, and didn't name a landing beach (Khor Fakkan is often cited but isn't in the source I read).
- **1973 oil shock (line 2).** The 1973–74 oil embargo roughly quadrupled crude prices. The Gulf Boom migration period is usually dated 1972–1983.
- **22 lakh, 4 in 5 in the Gulf, ₹2,16,893 crore (line 5).** Kerala Migration Survey 2023 (IIMAD/GIFT): 2.2 million emigrants; 80.5% in GCC countries (89.2% in 2018); total remittances ₹2,16,893 crore in 2023. Note the remittance figure is from all countries, so the line says "came home", not "came from the Gulf".
- **One rupee in four (line 6).** KMS 2023 puts remittances at 23.2% of Kerala's NSDP, so "about one in four" is a fair round. (For scale: Kerala's 2024–25 budget spending excluding debt repayment is ₹1,84,327 crore, so remittances are bigger than that. I left this out because it depends on which budget figure you use.)
- **Kuwait 1990 (line 7).** About 1,70,000 Indians evacuated, 488 flights, 13 Aug – 20 Oct 1990, Air India with Indian Airlines and the IAF; a Guinness record. Sources don't give an exact Kerala share, so the line says "many Malayalis", not "most".
- **Line 9.** Kerala had high educated unemployment in the 1970s–80s; this line is the what-if, not a claim about a number.
- **"Gulf houses", "Gulf gold", waiting mothers.** Cultural shorthand, not factual claims.
