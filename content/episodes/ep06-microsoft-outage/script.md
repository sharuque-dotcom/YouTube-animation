# Episode 6: The Day Email Stopped (Microsoft's Global Outage)

**[cold open]**
Thursday, November 12th, a little after 11 in the morning in New York. Somewhere inside Microsoft's cloud, servers start dropping out.
*A dark server hall; rows of status lights glowing blue. One light flips red, then the next.*

Within about an hour, people in 74 countries were reporting that Outlook, Teams and other Microsoft services had stopped working.
*A dark world map; red outage pings ripple out from city after city.*

You couldn't send an email. You couldn't book, move or cancel a meeting.
*A laptop on a desk: Outlook spinning, then "Can't connect"; a calendar invite stamped "Couldn't save".*

The next morning, before the stock market had even opened, Microsoft's shares were already falling.
*Dawn over a stock-exchange facade; a ticker blinks "MSFT −2.8% · PRE-MARKET".*

**[chapter 1 · The Day the Inbox Died]**
It wasn't just email. Outlook, Teams, OneDrive, SharePoint and Copilot were all hit.
*App icons line up like a row of shop signs; their lights flicker and go out one by one.*

On the outage tracker Downdetector, reports for Outlook alone peaked at around 140,000.
*A spike climbs off the top of a chart and keeps going.*

At its worst, there were nearly 20,000 new reports every minute, compared with fewer than 15 on a normal Thursday.
*Two counters side by side: "15 per minute" (a normal day) and "19,650 per minute", spinning up.*

---

**[chapter 2 · What Broke]**
Microsoft said there was "elevated load across multiple regions". A number of servers became unexpectedly unavailable, which put more strain on the ones still running.
*Racks fall dark one by one; the remaining ones glow hotter, heat shimmering.*

Some servers were failing automated health checks. And when Microsoft brought them back, they could become unresponsive again as soon as traffic hit them.
*A server reboots, its lights go green, a wave of traffic hits it, and it goes red again: a loop.*

Engineers patched a part of the system that handles sign-ins and routing traffic.
*A glowing patch slides into a server; a "routing" diagram re-wires itself.*

Microsoft said it saw no sign of a cyberattack. Australia's home affairs minister said Microsoft believed it was more like the 2024 CrowdStrike meltdown: an update gone wrong.
*A shield with "NO CYBERATTACK"; then a 2024 calendar with blue error screens on airport boards.*

The real cause, Microsoft says, will come in its post-incident report.
*A sealed envelope labelled "POST-INCIDENT REPORT · COMING".*

---

**[chapter 3 · A World Without Email]**
In Japan, government ministries struggled to email each other.
*Night in Tokyo: ministry windows lit, envelopes stuck mid-air between buildings.*

In Britain, the NHS told patients to keep their appointments, and some police forces asked people to use phone lines instead of online forms.
*A hospital entrance in the rain with a sign: "Please attend your appointment"; a police station with a ringing phone.*

The shipping giant Maersk and the stock-market operator Euronext reported problems. Luxury group Richemont's results were delayed, and Workhorse postponed its earnings.
*Container ships with a stalled email icon; an earnings slide stamped "DELAYED".*

Airlines in India rerouted customer emails. In the US, cities like Austin, Jacksonville and Baltimore County lost email; Austin said its 911 service was not affected.
*A runway at night; city halls with dark inbox icons; a 911 sign stays lit.*

Even AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic reported problems with tools that connect to Microsoft.
*Glowing AI chat bubbles whose links to Microsoft snap.*

**[chapter 4 · The Fix That Didn't Work]**
Early on Friday, Microsoft reported "early signs of stabilization", starting in Asia-Pacific.
*Dawn light reaches a map of Asia first; server lights turn green there.*

To protect messages stuck in limbo, it extended the email retry window to 48 hours, so delayed emails wouldn't simply be lost.
*Envelopes queue on a conveyor; a big "48 h" timer starts.*

But later it admitted that one fix was "not working as expected", and gave no timeline for a full recovery.
*A green light flickers back to red; a progress bar stalls at 70%.*

---

**[chapter 5 · Before the Bell]**
At 9:30 a.m., the New York Stock Exchange opens. But trading starts earlier, in what's called pre-market.
*Dawn on Wall Street; a clock ticks toward 9:30; traders' screens wake up early.*

On Friday morning, Microsoft's shares were down almost three percent before the bell, at around 508 dollars, while futures for the wider tech market barely moved.
*A ticker: MSFT falls red; next to it, "NASDAQ futures ≈ 0%" stays flat.*

For a company worth nearly four trillion dollars, a fall like that wipes out roughly 100 billion dollars of value, on paper.
*A tall glass tower of value; its top floors dim, with "≈ −$100B on paper".*

Microsoft is selling the world on AI and its cloud. On Thursday, that same cloud struggled to deliver an email.
*A Copilot-style glowing assistant bubble next to a frozen envelope.*
