**[cold open]**
Wednesday night, November 11th, 2026. In Santa Clara, the lights are still on in a boardroom.
*Night, rain: a glass boardroom high in a dark office tower; one floor still lit.*

Eleven directors. One question.
*Inside: a long table, eleven chairs filled with silhouettes, one leather jacket draped over the chair at the head.*

Should the man who built Nvidia still be allowed to run it?
*The jacket in a pool of light; the camera pushes in.*

In just a few days, investors had wiped out hundreds of billions of dollars of the company's value. And betting markets gave him less than a one-in-eight chance of surviving the year.
*A red market ticker scrolls across the rain-streaked glass; a betting gauge needle swings to 88% "OUT".*

**[chapter 1 · Previously]**
If you missed part one, here is what happened.
*A case board in the dark lights up, pin by pin.*

On Sunday, Jensen Huang admitted in an email to staff that he had been unfaithful to his wife.
*The email from part one, glowing on a phone.*

The woman, according to reports, was Alice Huang, who US intelligence suspected of working for China, and whose former company had bought billions of dollars of Nvidia chips.
*A faceless silhouette card under a scanning lens; a chip tower stamped "$4.8B · per NYT".*

Nvidia's board opened an investigation, and said it had no reason to believe any secrets were compromised.
*A boardroom door closes with "INVESTIGATION" on it.*

Then, on Wednesday, came a second report.
*A newspaper slaps onto a wet doorstep at night.*

---

**[chapter 2 · The Second Report]**
The New York Times reported that Huang had also had a romantic relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of health care, who reported directly to him.
*An org chart: a line from the CEO box down to "VP, Healthcare"; the line glows red.*

The paper cited five former employees. Nvidia and Powell did not respond to requests for comment.
*Five faceless silhouettes step into a pool of light; two "no comment" chips.*

When the Times called Huang himself, he reportedly said: "I don't think you should be talking to me directly." Then he hung up.
*A phone on a dark desk: "Call ended · 0:07".*

A CEO dating someone who reports to him is a serious problem at almost any big company, even before the questions about spies.
*A rulebook glows: "CONFLICT OF INTEREST"; a red flag rises.*

---

**[chapter 3 · The Board]**
Under Nvidia's rules, a majority of its eleven directors can remove the CEO, with or without cause. Every director except Huang is independent.
*Eleven glowing seats; ten switch to "independent", one stays "CEO"; a vote meter appears with "6 needed".*

The board hired the law firm Wachtell Lipton, and reportedly a former federal prosecutor, to investigate.
*Case files slide onto the table; a magnifier sweeps.*

Bloomberg reported it was also working with an outside firm to find possible replacements. Nvidia has no public succession plan.
*Empty name plates slide along the table: "?", "?", "?".*

Some analysts have named the chief financial officer, Colette Kress, as a possible interim chief.
*One name plate flips: "INTERIM?".*

---

**[chapter 4 · The Money]**
Investors did not wait for the decision.
*A trading-floor wall of screens at night, all turning red.*

Nvidia's shares fell about seven percent over the week, then more than eight percent more in after-hours trading on Wednesday.
*A line chart falls off a cliff; the screen flickers.*

If that drop holds, the company will have lost roughly 600 billion dollars in value since the investigation began.
*A counter rolls to −$600,000,000,000; banknotes blow away into the rain.*

Rivals rose: AMD gained four percent, Broadcom three.
*Two small green towers rise beside a cracked green chip tower.*

And Nvidia reports its quarterly results on November 17th, giving the board very little time.
*A calendar page: 17 NOV, with a ticking clock.*

**[chapter 5 · Washington]**
In Washington, lawmakers threatened to subpoena Huang if he refuses a closed-door briefing.
*The Capitol at night, searchlights; a subpoena flies onto a desk.*

After last week's midterm elections, Democrats now control both houses of Congress, and are promising oversight.
*A seat map flips colour, chamber by chamber.*

Inside the government, the Treasury Secretary reportedly challenged the Commerce Department over why Megaspeed had not been labelled a possible national-security threat.
*Two government buildings at night, with a crackle of tension between their windows.*

And President Trump said Huang told him he had no idea the woman was allegedly a spy.
*A phone line stretching to the White House; a speech bubble: "no idea".*
