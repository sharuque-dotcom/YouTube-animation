# Episode 4: The $129 Million Deepfake Heist

Toppled Giants · DRAFT for approval · facts as of 11 November 2026 · about 6 minutes
All claims about the three defendants are allegations from a US indictment; they are presumed innocent.
Each line has its act-it-out visual in *italics*.

**[cold open]**
The phone rings in the accounting department. It's the CEO.
*A bright office; a desk phone lights up with "CEO calling"; an employee's hand hovers.*

The voice is exactly right: the accent, the pauses, the way he says your name.
*A voice waveform pours out of the handset and settles into the shape of a face.*

He needs help with a confidential deal, and a lawyer from a big London firm will call with the details.
*A "CONFIDENTIAL" folder slides across the desk; a second phone lights up: "Lawyer, London".*

The money is wired. Millions of dollars, gone in minutes.
*A transfer bar fills; money flies off the screen as a stream of coins.*

The CEO never called. According to US prosecutors, the voice was made by artificial intelligence.
*The face in the waveform glitches into a wireframe mesh, then into code.*

And it allegedly happened again, and again, at thousands of companies around the world, for about 129 million dollars.
*A world map lights up with hundreds of ringing phones; a counter rolls to $129,000,000.*

**[chapter 1 · A Voice You Can Copy]**
Not long ago, faking someone's voice took a skilled impressionist. Now, with AI, a short recording can be enough.
*An impressionist on a tiny stage fades out; a 10-second audio clip feeds into a machine that prints a voice.*

Bosses are easy to record. They give interviews, earnings calls and conference talks.
*A CEO silhouette on a stage, microphones, a podcast mic, and a video player, all feeding a "voice sample" jar.*

In 2019, criminals cloned the voice of an energy company's boss and talked a manager into sending about 243,000 dollars.
*A 2019 calendar; a phone call; a small money bag flying off.*

In 2024, a worker at the engineering firm Arup in Hong Kong joined a video call with what looked like senior colleagues. Every person on the call was a deepfake. The company lost about 25 million dollars.
*A video-call grid of four faces; one by one each face peels back to show a wireframe; 15 transfer arrows fly out.*

---

**[chapter 2 · The Playbook]**
In November 2026, prosecutors in New York unsealed charges against three men. They say the scheme ran from July 2024 to February 2026.
*A courthouse in Manhattan; a sealed envelope opens; three faceless silhouettes slide in with name cards.*

First, an email that seemed to come from the company's CEO, asking for help with a secret transaction.
*An inbox: a message from "CEO" with a lock icon and "Confidential".*

Then a call, with the CEO's voice, and sometimes his face, made with AI.
*The phone call becomes a video call; the CEO's face glows with a faint digital shimmer.*

Then the CEO handed over to a "lawyer" at a major international law firm. The lawyer was fake too.
*A baton passes from the CEO tile to a "lawyer" tile with a fancy letterhead; a red "FAKE" tag appears.*

Prosecutors say one of the men, Jacob Soall, mostly played British lawyers, and kept a handwritten script titled "Lawyer Call".
*A notebook page under a desk lamp, handwriting typing itself: "LAWYER CALL".*

The lawyer gave the bank details. The employee sent the money.
*Account numbers type onto a form; a big SEND button is pressed.*

---

**[chapter 3 · The Victims]**
Prosecutors say thousands of companies were targeted.
*A wall of office buildings; thousands of tiny phones ringing inside them.*

A hospital system in Missouri lost about 4.8 million dollars. A financial technology company in the UK lost about 11.6 million.
*A hospital building with a red loss counter; a fintech office in London with a bigger counter.*

A hospital system in California, a bank in Rhode Island, a mutual fund company in Massachusetts, and even a pet food company in Florida were also named.
*Pins drop on a US map one by one, each with a little icon: a hospital, a bank, a fund chart, a dog bowl.*

**[chapter 4 · Follow the Money]**
The money was sent to bank accounts in Spain, Greece, the United Arab Emirates, Singapore and China.
*Glowing lines shoot from the US and UK to five pins on a world map.*

Then, prosecutors say, it was turned into cryptocurrency to hide where it came from, and split between the members of the group.
*Banknotes melt into crypto coins that scatter into three wallets.*

Two of the men, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit, are accused of laundering the money.
*Two faceless silhouettes behind a washing machine full of spinning coins.*

According to the indictment, Hazan boasted of laundering more than 400 million dollars in three years, and of being "number one all over the world".
*A gold trophy reading "#1" spins; a counter rolls to $400M with "ALLEGED" stamped on it.*

The men also allegedly used nicknames: "Rocky Balboa", "Superman Cash" and "Fat Boy".
*Three name tags pop onto the silhouettes: a boxing glove, a cape, a cartoon belly.*

---

**[chapter 5 · The Arrests]**
Soall was arrested in Spain in June 2026. Hazan was arrested in Spain in August. Sheetrit was arrested in the UK in September.
*A calendar flips June, August, September; police lights flash over Madrid and London skylines.*

They face charges including wire fraud, money laundering and aggravated identity theft. The most serious counts carry up to 20 years in prison.
*A charge sheet types itself; a "20 YEARS MAX" bar fills.*

The United States has asked for all three to be extradited. They are presumed innocent, and their lawyers did not comment.
*Three planes on a dotted line toward New York; a "PRESUMED INNOCENT" chip.*

---

**[chapter 6 · Don't Be Next]**
Prosecutors and the FBI say criminals are learning to "weaponize" new technology faster than companies can keep up.
*A shield flickers as glitchy AI particles hit it.*

The defences are simple. Hang up, and call back on a number you already know. Use a code word. Never let one person send a big payment alone.
*Three checklist cards land: a phone with a return arrow, a padlock with a word, two hands on one button.*

And be careful when a request is both urgent and secret. That is the oldest trick in the book.
*A stopwatch and a "SECRET" folder merge into a red warning light.*

## Fact check (sources)
- Defendants (Jacob Soall 41, Sharon Hazan 54, Guy Sheetrit 42), indictment unsealed 6 Nov 2026 in SDNY, July 2024 – Feb 2026, thousands of companies, about $129M, CEO emails about confidential deals, AI voice/image of CEOs and lawyers, the "Lawyer Call" script, named victims, crypto conversion, seven counts (up to 20 years; aggravated identity theft carries 2 years), Hazan's 2008 conviction, nicknames: [AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/3-charged-in-129-million-ai-scheme-targeting-thousands-of-companies), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/3-foreign-nationals-allegedly-used-ai-mimic-ceos-129m-scheme-doj-says-rcna607818).
- Arrests (Soall in Spain in June, Hazan in Spain in August, Sheetrit in the UK in September), extradition requested, Missouri hospital about $4.8M, UK fintech about $11.6M, accounts in Spain, Greece, the UAE, Singapore and China: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/us-charges-three-ai-ceo-impersonation-129m-fraud), with arrest dates confirmed in other reports.
- Hazan's ">$400M in three years" and "number one all over the world" boasts: NBC News quoting the indictment. Kept as an allegation.
- 2019 voice-clone case (about $243,000): widely reported (WSJ, 2019) and also cited by The Next Web.
- Arup, Hong Kong 2024 (HK$200M, about $25.6M, 15 transfers, everyone on the call faked): CNN / Fortune / Hong Kong police reports.
- FBI's "weaponize emerging technologies" line: AP quoting the FBI's James C. Barnacle Jr.
- Wording choices:
  - Sources differ on Hazan's gender, so the script uses no pronoun for Hazan.
  - The California hospital and Rhode Island bank amounts differ between sources, so no figures are given for them.
  - The "short recording can be enough" line is a general claim about voice cloning, not specific to this case.
