# Episode 6 · How Jensen Huang Lost Nvidia in 14 Days

Toppled Giants · DRAFT for approval · facts as of 12 November 2026 · about 9 minutes
Stands alone: a viewer who saw neither earlier Nvidia video can follow it.
Real people appear as faceless silhouettes (episode 3 rule); the leather jacket stands in for Huang.
Reported claims stay attributed. Nvidia gave no reason for the resignation, and the script never says it did.

Title options:
1. How Jensen Huang Lost Nvidia in 14 Days
2. Why Nvidia's Founder Just Resigned
3. The Email That Ended Jensen Huang

---

**[cold open]**
Thursday morning, November 12th, 2026. A short document lands on a government website.
*Night turning to grey dawn: a filing sheet slides out of a glowing slot; "FORM 8-K" lights up.*

It says that Jensen Huang has resigned as president and chief executive of Nvidia. Effective immediately.
*The words "EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY" burn in red; the camera pushes in.*

No reason is given.
*An empty line on the page under a scanning lens: "Reason: —".*

Thirty-three years. A company worth around four trillion dollars. Gone in one paragraph.
*The leather jacket lifts off the head chair of the boardroom and the chair is left empty in the key light.*

Fourteen days earlier, almost nobody saw it coming. This is how it happened.
*A calendar flips backwards from 12 November to 29 October.*

---

**[chapter 1 · The Builder]**
In 1993, three engineers met at a Denny's diner in California and sketched out a company. One of them was thirty years old. His name was Jensen Huang.
*The diner at night in the rain, a warm window; inside, three silhouettes and a napkin sketch of a chip.*

For years, their chips made video games look better. Then researchers found the same chips were perfect for training artificial intelligence.
*The napkin chip becomes a real chip; game pixels flow in, a glowing neural web flows out.*

When the AI boom arrived, almost every major AI system on Earth needed Nvidia.
*A city of data centres lights up block by block, every cable leading to one green tower.*

The company became one of the most valuable in history. And Huang, in his black leather jacket, ran it his own way. Dozens of executives reported directly to him.
*A wheel: one hub in a leather jacket, dozens of spokes.*

Think of a wheel with one hub. It turns beautifully. Until something happens to the hub.
*The wheel spins; a hairline crack appears at the centre.*

---

**[chapter 2 · The Customer]**
Nvidia's best chips are not just products. Governments treat them like weapons.
*A chip in a museum-style glass case with a guard's torch beam across it.*

Since 2022, the United States has restricted sales of its most advanced AI chips to China.
*A dam wall rises across a dark river of chips; the Chinese side goes dry.*

So investigators began to ask whether chips were leaking around the wall, through companies in Southeast Asia.
*Thin glowing streams find their way around the end of the dam.*

One company drew attention. Megaspeed, a cloud firm in Singapore. The New York Times reported that it bought more than 4.8 billion dollars of Nvidia chips in two years.
*A Singapore dock at night; crates stack into a tower; a tag reads "$4.8B · per NYT".*

For part of 2023, its managing director was a woman named Alice Huang. The two share a surname. They are not reported to be related.
*Two name cards side by side, a "not related" chip between them.*

Megaspeed denies any wrongdoing, and says inspections found no violations.
*A stamp: "DENIES"; three inspection ticks.*

---

**[chapter 3 · Day One]**
October 29th. The New York Times reports that American intelligence agencies had been watching a person close to Jensen Huang, because they believed she could be working for China.
*A newspaper slaps onto a wet doorstep; the calendar reads "DAY 1".*

The Financial Times reports the relationship was romantic. The Wall Street Journal reports that intelligence services in three countries had reason to suspect her.
*Three more papers land, one by one, each closer to the camera.*

Nvidia pushes back. It says Huang talks to thousands of customers every year.
*A corporate statement slides out; the lens scans it.*

But in Washington, three committee chairmen ask Huang for a closed-door briefing.
*The Capitol at night under searchlights; a letter with three seals.*

For ten days, he says nothing in public.
*The calendar pages turn in silence: Day 2, Day 5, Day 9. A phone lights up, unanswered.*

---

**[chapter 4 · The Email]**
Then, on Sunday, November 8th, Nvidia's staff receive an email from their founder.
*Thousands of phones light up across a dark campus, window by window.*

"I was unfaithful to my wife," he writes. "My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did."
*The quote types itself on one phone.*

He says he supports an investigation and will cooperate fully.
*A door marked "INVESTIGATION" opens.*

The board says it has no reason to believe that national security, or Nvidia's secrets, were compromised. And it says Huang will stay on.
*Eleven chairs; a banner "CEO CONTINUES"; the jacket still on the head chair.*

On Monday, the stock falls about four percent. President Trump calls Huang "an honorable guy."
*A red ticker dips; a quote card.*

For a moment, it looks like he might survive.
*The crack in the wheel stops growing. Hold.*

**[chapter 5 · The Second Report]**
On Tuesday, the board hires a former top federal prosecutor and one of Wall Street's most powerful law firms to investigate.
*A briefcase clicks open on the boardroom table; a magnifying lamp swings over files.*

Then, on Wednesday, the New York Times publishes again.
*A second newspaper lands on the first.*

It reports that Huang also had a relationship with Kimberly Powell, a senior Nvidia executive who reported directly to him. The paper cites five former employees. Nvidia and Powell did not comment.
*An org chart: the line from the CEO box to one box below glows red.*

When the Times called Huang, he reportedly said, "I don't think you should be talking to me directly." And hung up.
*A phone on a dark desk: "Call ended · 0:07".*

One scandal was about a customer. This one was about his own company. Almost every big firm has rules about a boss dating someone who works for him.
*Two red flags rise: "CUSTOMER", then "EMPLOYEE".*

That evening, the Wall Street Journal reports the board is preparing to remove him. In Santa Clara, the directors meet into the night.
*The glass boardroom in the rain, one floor lit; the crack in the wheel runs to the rim.*

---

**[chapter 6 · Day Fourteen]**
Thursday morning, the announcement arrives. Huang has resigned as president, as chief executive, and from the board.
*Dawn; the filing from the cold open, now in full.*

Nvidia's finance chief, Colette Kress, becomes interim CEO. And for the first time in its history, Nvidia names a chairman of the board, Harvey Jones.
*The head chair turns; a new nameplate slides in: "INTERIM CEO". A second, taller chair appears: "CHAIR".*

Wall Street reacts fast. The stock closed on Wednesday near 174 dollars. It opened on Thursday at 151, a fall of about thirteen percent.
*A cliff: the price line walks off the edge, 174 to 151.*

Then something strange happens. The stock climbs back, and ends the morning down only around five.
*The line catches a ledge and climbs.*

Because the question investors feared most had finally been answered.
*Fog clears from the chart.*

---

**[outro · What we know]**
So what do we actually know?
*An evidence board in the dark; cards light one by one.*

Huang admitted being unfaithful. That is confirmed. He resigned. That is confirmed.
*Two green "CONFIRMED" stamps.*

The spying claims come from unnamed intelligence sources in news reports. No one has been charged. And the board has said it has no reason to believe any secrets were lost.
*Amber "REPORTED" tags; an empty "CHARGES" card.*

Huang is still Nvidia's largest individual shareholder, with about 3.6 percent. The investigation is still running. And on Tuesday, Nvidia reports its earnings, for the first time without him.
*A single share certificate; a calendar ring around 17 November; an empty stage with one spotlight.*

For thirty-three years, people asked what could possibly stop Nvidia. A better chip. A trade war. A crash.
*Three threats bounce off the green tower.*

In the end, it was none of those. It was an email.
*The phone from chapter 4, alone in the dark. The screen goes off.*

To settle before recording:

- Powell's exact title (vice president vs senior vice president): script avoids it.
- "Around four trillion dollars": use the value at Wednesday's close.
- "Ends the morning down around five": update to the actual close on Thursday.
- "The question investors feared most had been answered" is our reading, not a reported fact. Keep it as narration, or cut.

Left out on purpose:
- Trump's Tim Cook suggestion and Musk's "honor" remark (single source each, and off the story's spine)
