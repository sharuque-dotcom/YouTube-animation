## English (for checking)

1. 1741. A Dutch governor threatens the king of Travancore: "Want a war?" The king replies: "I've been thinking of invading Europe one day!"
2. People laughed. Why? You see, the Dutch were then the world's biggest trading empire.
3. They landed on the beach at Colachel. Cannons. Guns. A European army.
4. And Marthanda Varma had? Nair soldiers... and the fishermen on the beach!
5. The story goes that the fishermen built fake wooden cannons, and swam out at night to sink Dutch ships.
6. The war dragged on for months. Then a cannonball landed in the Dutch gunpowder. Boom!
7. 10 August. The Dutch surrendered. Their captain, De Lannoy, was taken prisoner.
8. Wait... what if the Dutch had won? Kerala could have been a Dutch colony for centuries, like Indonesia.
9. But here's the real twist: the prisoner trained Travancore's army, built its forts, and became the "Valiya Kappithan" (Great Captain).
10. It's like making the losing team's captain your own coach!
11. He served the king for 36 years, and he still rests in Udayagiri Fort.
12. The king never captured Europe... but he captured Europe's captain!

## Fact check (sources below)

- **The threat and the reply.** Van Imhoff (Dutch governor of Ceylon) threatened war, and Marthanda Varma replied that he "had been thinking about invading Europe some day." The popular version adds "with fishing boats," but I couldn't find a source for that detail, so I left it out.
- **The fishermen.** Fake cannons are a local legend, so the script says "the story goes." Fishermen divers sinking Dutch ships at night is cited on Wikipedia, sourced to Onmanorama.
- **The gunpowder hit.** A Travancore cannonball hit the Dutch gunpowder store. Some accounts call this a legend; the Wikipedia battle article reports it as fact.
- **The date.** The battle was on 10 August 1741. Dutch records date the surrender to 7 August, so the script says only "ഓഗസ്റ്റ് പത്ത്" (10 August).
- **De Lannoy.**
  - Captured with about 24 Dutch officers.
  - Became "Valiya Kappithan".
  - Rebuilt Udayagiri fort and built the Travancore Lines.
  - Died 1 June 1777 and is buried in the chapel at Udayagiri Fort.
  - His tombstone says he served "nearly thirty-seven years." 1741 to 1777 is 36 years, so the script says മുപ്പത്താറ് (36).
- **The what-if line.** Indonesia was under the Dutch East India Company and then Dutch rule from the 1600s until 1949. The comparison is our what-if, not a claim.
- **Avoided on purpose.** The "first Asian victory over a European power" line. The sources don't support it as stated.
