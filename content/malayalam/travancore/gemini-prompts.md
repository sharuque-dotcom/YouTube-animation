## STYLE GUIDE (paste at the end of every prompt)

> **Style:** 2.5D pixel-art diorama. A miniature 3D set built from simple boxes and cylinders, covered in crisp hand-made pixel-art textures (about 24 texels per metre, nearest-neighbour, no smoothing). Characters are flat 2D pixel sprites (about 34×50 pixels, chunky, with a 1-pixel dark outline) standing in the 3D world like paper cut-outs. They receive light and cast real shadows.
> **Camera:** always looking down from a high three-quarter angle, never at eye level. Slow, smooth, weighty moves with no overshoot: a slow push-in, a lateral glide or a rising pull-back. Tilt-shift lens: one sharp horizontal focus band on the subject, with strong blur above and below, so the scene feels like a tabletop miniature.
> **Light:** light is the subject. One large ambient key light with practical lights (lamps, windows, fire, the sun) placed in the scene, casting soft real shadows. Two colour temperatures: a big ambient tone plus small warm practical lights. Heavy bloom and soft halation around every light source, a gentle vignette, and filmic tone mapping. The darkest colour is a warm ink, never pure black. No film grain.
> **Motion:** the characters animate in stepped, low-frame-rate pixel animation (2-frame idles, 4-frame walks at 6–10 fps). Everything else (camera, light, water, rain, smoke, particles, focus) moves perfectly smoothly. The set itself never shakes.
> **Atmosphere:** light haze or fog with one colour per scene, depth-scaled particles (dust, rain or sea spray, where near is bigger and faster), and small story details (a lit window, distant lights).
> **Setting:** Kerala, India, 1947: red terracotta-tiled pitched roofs, lime-washed and laterite walls, wooden verandah pillars, coconut palms, people in white mundus.
> **Avoid:** voxel or cubic characters, eye-level shots, wipes, spins, fast cuts, pure black, text, watermarks.
