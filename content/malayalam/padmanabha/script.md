## English check

1. 2011. When a temple's vaults were opened, they found treasure valued at over ₹1 lakh crore! But one door is still shut... What if Vault B is opened?
2. You see: Thiruvananthapuram, Sree Padmanabhaswamy temple. In 1750 Marthanda Varma dedicated his whole kingdom to the deity. From then on the kings ruled as Padmanabhadasa, servants of Padmanabha. Centuries of offerings slept in the vaults.
3. T.P. Sundararajan. A man who left the IPS to become a lawyer. He went to court asking whether the temple's wealth was safe.
4. People laughed: "An old lawyer, taking on the royal family?"
5. June 2011. The Supreme Court ordered it. Five of the six vaults were opened.
6. A four-foot gold Mahavishnu idol. An 18-foot gold chain. 800 kg of gold coins. Gold coconut shells studded with gems!
7. Like opening the old paddy chest at the tharavad and finding gold instead of rice!
8. Wait... and Vault B? The iron grille came off. The wooden door opened. Only the third iron door would not move. The devaprashnam said: do not open it.
9. Three weeks later, Sundararajan died. People said: a curse! There were even stories that the Arabian Sea lay behind the door.
10. But here's the twist. Vinod Rai's report said Vault B had been opened seven times before! The royal family says only the small front chamber was opened. No one has seen the main chamber to this day.
11. In 2020 the Supreme Court left the decision to the temple committee. Last year the committee left it to the tantri. Still no decision.
12. What if that door is opened? Maybe a treasure bigger than the other five. Maybe nothing. But for lakhs of devotees it isn't treasure, it is the Lord's own property.
13. Until then... the serpent on the door keeps guard. The only door still unopened.

## Fact check

- **1750 dedication.** The ruler dedicated the kingdom to the deity in 1750 and the royals ruled as "Padmanabha Dasa" (IANS via Lokmat, Aug 2025, which calls him "Anizham Thirunal", i.e. Anizham Thirunal Marthanda Varma). Line 2's "centuries of offerings" fits Wikipedia: donations from Cheras, Pandyas, Travancore royals, Kolathiris and others.
- **Sundararajan.** Former IPS officer (1963 exam, IB and Home Ministry, resigned), enrolled as an advocate in 1970; born 20 Apr 1941, died 17 Jul 2011 in Thiruvananthapuram aged 70, fever then heart attack (Wikipedia; India TV: "the former IPS officer who became a lawyer"). The SC ordered the vaults opened on his petition (India TV). "Old lawyer" fits: he was 70. **The mocking quote is dramatisation**, not a sourced quote. He was also on the SC's inspection panel.
- **"Three weeks later"**: vaults opened 27/30 June 2011 (Wikipedia gives both dates), death 17 July 2011, so about 2.5–3 weeks. The script says "people said: a curse" as a belief, not as fact. His death was fever and a heart attack.
- **Five of six.** A, C, D, E, F opened; B unopened (Wikipedia, The News Minute). Vaults G and H were found later by the amicus (2014) and are also closed, so "six" is the commonly cited count. "The only door still unopened" in line 13 is slightly loose given G and H. **Check**: keep as drama, or change to "ആ ഒരു വാതിൽ".
- **₹1 lakh crore.** A widely reported media estimate ("more than Rs 100,000 crores", TNM); no official valuation was ever published, and the full inventory is not public. Line 1 says "valued at over", which matches the reporting.
- **Items.** Wikipedia's list (from news reports, not an official inventory): 4 ft x 3 ft solid gold Mahavishnu idol studded with diamonds, an 18-foot gold chain, an 800 kg hoard of gold coins, gold coconut shells studded with rubies and emeralds. Vinod Rai also cites the 800 kg box of gold coins.
- **Vault B doors.** Committee removed the metal grille, opened a wooden door, then "a third door made of iron, which was jammed shut" (Wikipedia). 2012 attempt: grille and two of three locks removed, third left alone; a gas cutter could have opened it "but we decided against it" (Onmanorama). 2011 Ashtamangala devaprashnam said it should not be opened (Wikipedia, Onmanorama).
- **Myths.** Cobra-like engraving on the door; the "curse" tied to Sundararajan's death; old story of "rushing water" and a link to the Arabian Sea (TNM myths piece). Script presents these as stories people tell.
- **Opened seven times.** Vinod Rai's 2014 report as amicus/auditor: opened twice in 1990 and five times in 2002 (Onmanorama citing the 1 Aug 2014 status report). Royal family: Vault B has "a small antechamber... The main chamber has never been opened" (Onmanorama).
- **2020 verdict.** 13 Jul 2020: SC upheld the royal family's shebait rights and did not order Vault B opened; the decision was left to the temple's administrative and advisory committees (Onmanorama 2020 and Aug 2025).
- **2025.** 7 Aug 2025 joint committee meeting: the state's representative raised opening Vault B, the others did not respond, and the committees asked the tantri to decide later (IANS via Lokmat; Onmanorama 8 Aug 2025). **Check**: I found no decision after Aug 2025. If anything changed in 2026, line 11 needs updating. "Last year" = 2025.

**Sensitivity choices.** No disrespect to the deity or the royal family; the curse and sea stories are framed as what people said; line 12 gives the devotees' view the last word before the callback. The deity is never drawn as a cartoon (see cast).
