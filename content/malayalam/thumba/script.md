## English check

1. 1963, Thiruvananthapuram. Rocket parts on the back of a bicycle! And they're heading into a church! What if that bishop had said "no"?
2. You see, rocket experiments need a spot on Earth's magnetic equator. That line runs right across Thumba beach. And in the middle stands a church.
3. Vikram Sarabhai and a few young men. No lab, no office, no money.
4. The world laughed: "Rockets, in a country with hunger?"
5. One Saturday, Sarabhai met Bishop Pereira. The bishop said: "Come to mass tomorrow."
6. Wait... On Sunday the bishop asked: "Children, can we give God's house to science?" The fisherfolk answered as one: "Amen!"
7. The prayer room became a lab. The bishop's house became an office. 183 families left their homes.
8. Like handing over your own house so the neighbour's kid can study!
9. 21 November, 6:25 in the evening. The rocket shot up! An orange cloud in the sky!
10. What if the bishop had said no? India's rocket would have been delayed by years. And the story of a young man named Kalam, who learned his craft in that church room, would have changed too.
11. Today that church is a space museum. The journey that began there reached the Moon and Mars.
12. One "Amen" that Sunday... that was India's first countdown.

## Fact check

- **Launch (lines 1, 9).** First sounding rocket, a two-stage NASA-supplied Nike-Apache, launched from Thumba on 21 Nov 1963 (ISRO diamond-jubilee note; Wikipedia TERLS). Time 6:25 pm and the sodium vapour cloud "tinted orange by the setting sun" are from The Better India only.
- **Bicycle (line 1).** The famous photo of a rocket part on a bicycle; The Better India says parts and payloads went "by bullock carts and bicycle". No source ties the photo to the exact 1963 launch, so the hook says "rocket part", not "this rocket". (The bullock-cart photo is usually linked to the 1981 APPLE satellite, so the script leaves the cart out.)
- **Magnetic equator (line 2).** Thumba lies very close to Earth's magnetic equator, ideal for studying the equatorial electrojet (Wikipedia, Better India).
- **Line 3.** Sarabhai led the effort with a young team (Kalam an early recruit, trained at NASA Wallops Island). "No money" is a dramatisation of a famously shoestring start.
- **Line 4 quote.** Dramatisation of the common "why rockets in a poor country" criticism; Sarabhai answered it publicly. Not a sourced quote.
- **Lines 5–6.** Kalam's account in Ignited Minds (quoted by Onmanorama): Sarabhai met Bishop Peter Bernard Pereira on a Saturday; the bishop asked him back next day; at the Sunday service he asked, "Children, can we give them God's abode for a scientific mission?" and got "a hearty Amen". Onmanorama notes some fisherfolk cried at the loss.
- **Line 7.** "The church's prayer room was the scientists' first laboratory and the bishop's quarters ... the first design and drawing office"; 183 families moved, new church built at Pallithura (Onmanorama; ThePrint quoting Kalam). Wikipedia says the first rockets were assembled in the former St Louis High School: the script avoids saying the rocket itself was assembled at the altar.
- **Line 10** is the speculative what-if, phrased as "would have".
- **Line 11.** The church became the VSSC Space Museum in 1985 (Onmanorama, ThePrint). ISRO reached the Moon (Chandrayaan-1 2008, Chandrayaan-3 landing 2023) and Mars orbit (Mangalyaan, 2014).
